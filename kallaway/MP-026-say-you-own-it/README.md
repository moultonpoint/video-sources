# MP-026 · Say you own it

Sources for my video about Kallaway and Sandcastles. Every claim in the video is listed here, with the second it appears, where it comes from, and a saved copy.

Evidence collected November 24–25, 2026 from logged-out sessions (one exception: the July 14 check in the TikTok app, noted below). Every source page has a copy on the Internet Archive from before the video was posted, so later edits to the originals don't change what's recorded here.

If I got something wrong, open an issue with the source. Corrections go in the log at the bottom.

## My own interest

The end of the video mentions a tool I built: my own /analyze, free and MIT licensed, no paid tier, no sign-up for the skill itself: [github.com/moultonpoint/skills](https://github.com/moultonpoint/skills). I make no money from it, and nobody paid me for this video. Sandcastles' Claude plugin also has an /analyze command, and mine overlaps with that video-analysis feature. In the video I say I started building my own (0:36) and that it's on "my GitHub" (1:32).

## TikTok label check: logged-out web data, all 38 posts

TikTok's public page for each post includes data about the post. A post with a disclosure label carries fields such as `isAd`, `adAuthorization` and `adLabelVersion`. For example, an #OldSpicePartner #ad post ([link](https://www.tiktok.com/@adamw/video/7519264757614972190)) has `isAd: true`, `adAuthorization: true`, `adLabelVersion: 2`.

All 38 of these posts have `isAd: false` and none of those fields, the same as the July 14 post, which showed no label in the app. The Internet Archive copies saved on Nov 25 carry the same data, so this can be checked without trusting me. Per-post data: [evidence/page-data/](evidence/page-data/), an extract of each page's post data (`null` means the field wasn't on the page). Re-run it with [tools/label_check.py](tools/label_check.py), which reads [posts.csv](posts.csv) and rewrites those files.

Limits: this is TikTok's web data, not the app. I found no public example of a "Promotional content" post to confirm that label uses the same fields as "Paid partnership". In the app, only July 14 was checked (no label visible on Nov 24); the other 37 are unchecked in the app.

## What this does not claim

- That Kallaway broke the law. Only a court decides that. The $2,017,344 is an illustration (one maximum penalty per post), and the video's answer to what he'll pay is "probably nothing."
- That he hides it. It's in his bio, he says it on podcasts and YouTube, and two posts flash his bio. The point is that the pitch itself doesn't say it.
- Anything about Instagram (it needs a login, so it wasn't checked) or deleted posts. Four old @kanekallaway posts from 2022–23 had no usable subtitles and weren't checked for speech.
- What his stake is. "Co-founder" and "we built the whole thing together" are his words.

## How it was checked

- **Speech:** TikTok's own subtitles for each post, checked against a local Whisper transcription. Quote times are subtitle windows.
- **On screen:** frames sampled from every post ("none seen" in posts.csv means none in the sampled frames). The Oct 26 post was read at two frames per second, start to finish.
- **Captions:** the full written caption of every post (in each page-data file as `desc`).
- **Finding the posts:** his public TikTok listing was pulled three times and combined by post ID. @kallaway.marketing gave 186, the same as his profile count.
- **Screenshots:** fresh logged-out browser, 2160 × 3840, unedited apart from the `-crop` files, which are pixel crops of the full images next to them.
